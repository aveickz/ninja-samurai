About the Game

The Last Samurai grew out of «Bang! The Samurai Sword», but it has drifted a long way from it. This is a saga of two clans at war — the samurai and the ninja; the disciplined code of honour of Bushido against the secretive, cunning path of the Shinobi. The game is played in the open: character cards and team allegiances are visible to everyone from the very start. Only the cards in your hand stay hidden.

Setup

Before the game begins, players work through four steps in order: they split into sides, take their seats, draw character cards, and set out their starting life and victory points. Then everyone draws seven cards from the deck — every starting hand is the same size.

Choosing sides

Sides are formed either by common agreement or by random draw. With an even number of players the teams are equal — half samurai, half ninja. With an odd number, one side gets an extra player; which side that is must be settled beforehand by common agreement.

The first turn of the game goes to a player chosen by lot; play then passes clockwise, and every new round begins with that same player.

Seating

Once sides are set, players take their places at the table. The intended seating alternates sides: samurai — ninja — samurai — ninja, and so on. By common agreement you may sit however you like, but the game was designed around alternating seats.

Drawing characters

All character cards are shuffled into one face-down pile, and each player draws one at random regardless of their role. The character card is placed face up in front of the player — its abilities will serve them for the whole game. The number inside the heart is the character’s maximum life: however much life is lost, missing life and “full health” are always counted from this number.

Setting out tokens

Each player sets out their starting life, as printed on their character card (the number inside the heart). By default every player also has four victory points. In modes with equal (even) teams this value is the same for everyone. For odd player counts, the starting victory points and the number of cards drawn in the draw phase are adjusted according to the tables below.

The Game

The game runs as a sequence of player turns — see «The Turn».

Even teams are best; with an odd number of players, see «Uneven Teams» at the end of the rules.

End of the game

The game runs until one of three conditions is met: the deck runs out — in which case the turn during which the last card was drawn is the final one, and victory points are counted once it ends; or one of the players loses every one of their victory points; or the game is ended early by common agreement — in which case it is customary to finish the current round up to the player seated before the one who begins the rounds, and then count victory points.

The Turn

A turn goes like this: at the start of the turn the player discards down to nine cards in hand; then they act; at the end of the turn they draw three cards from the deck, after which every player on the opposing team draws one card.

On their turn a player may: make up to two weapon attacks against any living player, play action and group cards, trade cards, set a Trap in front of themselves, apply Effects, and change their Stance or Aura.

Between turns the hand is unlimited: having drawn at the end of the turn, a player keeps as many cards as they like and decides what to discard only before their next turn starts — already knowing everything that has happened at the table.

Death

A player who loses their last life is dead until the end of the turn in which they were killed. The victory point goes to the killer; if poison did the killing, the point goes to the discard pile. The dead player's Stance, Aura, Trap and Effects are discarded. While a player is dead nobody can touch them: they cannot be attacked or targeted by cards and Effects. From the next turn on, everyone who died in the previous turn comes back to life and can be attacked again. At the start of their next own turn the revived player goes through the recovery phase — see «Drawing Cards».

Drawing Cards

Cards are drawn at the end of your own turn: once you have finished acting, you take three cards from the deck as a baseline. After that every player on the opposing team draws one card.

In modes with unequal teams, players on the smaller side draw one extra card in the draw phase — exact values are in the tables in the «Uneven Teams» chapter at the end of the rules.

The recovery phase happens at the start of your turn if you have been dead: instead of the usual discard down to nine, you may first discard any number of cards from your hand and then draw new ones, so that you end with no more than seven cards in hand. A player killed by poison gets no recovery — see «Poison».

During your turn you may discard two cards of the same type — Weapon, Trap, Stance, Defense, Effect, Intervention, Modifier or group card — to draw one new card from the deck. Or you may discard two identical cards to draw two new cards from the deck.

At the start of your turn you cannot hold more than nine cards — you discard the surplus, choosing which cards go, before your first action. Between turns the hand is unlimited.

Weapons and Attack

On your turn you may make at most two weapon attacks against other players. To attack you need a Weapon card. Every Weapon card states the highest complexity it can handle and its attack power. Dead players cannot be targeted.

Attack complexity

Every player has a complexity — the number an attack on them must beat. By default every player's complexity is 1; seating does not affect it.

The number on a Weapon card is the highest complexity that weapon handles. The attack goes through if the weapon's complexity is greater than or equal to the target's current complexity. Thrown weapons handle any complexity.

Cards, Effects, Stances and Auras may shift complexity either way — both the defender's complexity and the one a weapon handles. «Attacks against you are 1 harder» means an attack on that player needs a weapon that handles one more point of complexity.

Complexity is worked out the moment the attack is declared, with every Effect, Stance and Aura in play at that moment.

The attacking player lays down the Weapon card and declares their target. The defender either takes the blow and suffers wounds equal to the attack power, or blocks the attack with a Defense card — see «Defense».

Attacks with thrown weapons do not trigger Traps. Weapons can also be strengthened with poison.
With no weapon in hand you may still attack bare-handed at complexity 1 with attack power 1, but this counts as both of your weapon attacks.

Modifier

Any weapon attack may be strengthened with a single special Modifier card with a red banner. The order in which positive and negative bonuses stack is described in detail in the «Order of Resolution» chapter.

Defense

The defender may either take the blow with dignity and suffer wounds equal to the attack power, or play a Defense card to block the attack. If a Trap lies in front of the defending player, it is their own choice whether to trigger it or to play a Defense card. If the attack is blocked successfully, none of the attacker's additional effects happen.

The defender's teammates may play their own Defense cards to absorb part of the damage — this counts as an Intervention. One such card brings the final number of wounds down to one; to take away that last wound as well, the team must discard two Defense cards in total. The cards' own special effects (return damage, poison and so on) do not fire.

If the defender cannot defend themselves — under a defenceless Effect, or against a weapon whose blow «cannot be defended against» — their allies cannot defend them either.

Thrust

Some cards deal wounds directly, bypassing the attack mechanic — this is a thrust (direct damage). Such cards are marked with a spearhead icon and the word «Thrust» in the description. A thrust is not an attack: it cannot be blocked with a Defense card, it does not trigger Traps and it does not use up an attack attempt. If a thrust finishes a player off, the victory point goes to whoever played the card. Anything that fires «on taking wounds» fires from a thrust just as it would from an attack.

Traps

On their turn any player may set at most one Trap in front of themselves, placing a special Trap card face down and standing the trap figurine on top of it. During your turn you may swap the active Trap in front of you for a new one from your hand as often as you like, taking the current one back into your hand.

When you are attacked, the Trap triggers if you do not intend to defend. If you play a Defense card, the Trap is left untouched. Thrown weapons and their attacks never trigger a Trap. On death you automatically discard your Trap card.

There are cards that reveal Traps — in that case the Trap card is turned face up, with the trap figurine left standing on it.

If revealing, stealing or triggering a Trap shows that the card used was not a proper Trap card, that player dies immediately and hands a victory point to whoever initiated the action. A Trap resolves even if the attack was lethal.

Stances

Once during your turn you may put a special Stance card into play. Place it beside your character card. Your Stance stays active until you die or lose it by some other particular means.

Only one Stance can be active at a time. If you already had an active Stance when you play a new one, you take the previous Stance card back into your hand — just like a Trap.

The active abilities of a Stance can be used immediately.

Effects

Much like the lingering action of poison, cards of a special type called Effects can be placed on a player. Effects are either permanent or one-shot. Permanent Effects last until the player dies, while one-shot Effects disappear once they fire. Most Effects are harmful.

Effects can be stolen, moved or removed with special incense-stick cards. Effects may be placed on any living player.

When an Effect card is played, it is placed face up to the right of that player's character card. A player may hold any number of Effects, but cannot hold two Effects of the same name.

On death a player loses every Effect and discards those cards.

Poison

Players can be poisoned, either by making poisoned weapon attacks or through the action of other cards. Cards that carry poison are marked with a special icon. A poisoned player is marked with the poison figurine placed on top of their role card. Poison does not blunt the blade: it has no effect on the poisoned player's attack power.

The three afflictions of the poisoned

  1. A poisoned player loses two life at the end of their turn. If that kills them, the victory point goes to the discard pile.
  2. A poisoned attack against an already poisoned player deals one extra wound.
  3. A player killed by poison gets no recovery phase: they discard their face-up cards like any dead player, but their hand stays exactly as it was — no discard, no drawing back up to seven.

Intervention

Intervention is a special card type that can be played outside your own turn. An Intervention card may be played in the middle of someone else's action — an attack, an Effect, any other card — or at any moment when nothing at all is happening at the table. This is the core mechanic for pulling players into events beyond their own turn.

When any player plays an Intervention card, an intervention transaction opens. While it is open, any player (including the key player) may add their own card to the shared open pool. Every card in the transaction counts as played simultaneously — neither speed nor the order of laying cards down matters. The transaction closes on its own once nobody wishes to add another Intervention.

When it resolves, the key player — the defender in a direct attack, or whoever's life the transaction has put at risk — decides the order and the manner in which the played cards apply, choosing the outcome most favourable to themselves. Every contradiction (simultaneous healing and wounding, a redirected attack and an added blow, an Effect applied and removed) is read in their favour.

Intervention is meant as a strategic decision, not a contest of reflexes — discussing, bargaining and thinking it over are all allowed.

Team Aura

Team Auras are powerful cards that bend the rules in favour of the team that played them. An Aura's effect reaches everyone at the table — allies and opponents alike — so active Auras have to be watched, and your own turns recalculated around them.

Any player may play an Aura on their turn: the card goes face up in front of the character, with the banner figurine on top. A player holds one Aura at most; a new one replaces the old, which returns to hand like a Stance or a Trap. Auras of different players on the same team do not cancel one another — they stack.

An Aura stays in play as long as its owner is alive and has not replaced it; when the owner dies it is discarded with the rest of their face-up cards.

Conditions

Many cards in the deck work differently depending on the situation at the table — who is playing them, what state that player is in, and what lies in front of them. Such cards carry conditional bonuses: the base effect always fires, and extra modifiers are added to it (or subtracted from it) when a particular condition is met. Conditions are marked out explicitly on the card — worth noticing while you are still reading it.

Faction bonuses. Some cards behave differently in the hands of a samurai and a ninja: one side gets a boost, the other a penalty (discarding a card, losing life, a restricted action). For example: a weapon whose blow a Ninja cannot defend against, while a Samurai pays for the attack with a discarded card.

Health context. A card may refer to two states of a player:

  • At death's door — the player has a single life left. This state opens up «desperate» bonuses: added damage, healing for allies, protective effects, risk and reward.
  • At full health — the player is at the maximum life printed on their character card. This usually brings out «forward» bonuses: extra card draw, stronger attacks, a wider set of actions.

Counting the table. Cards with the counter icon scale with what is visible on the table at the moment they are played: for each poisoned player, for each enemy Trap, for each living ally. Only what lies face up is counted, and only once — at the moment the card is played.

Named condition. A card with the faceless-bust icon names a character: Taka, Favourite weapon: Norio. Such a line works only for the named player — the character card lies face up in front of them, so the check is a glance at the table. In anyone else's hands the card stays ordinary; the named line is simply not read.

Conditions on poison, Stances and other circumstances exist too — they are written right on the card and read exactly as written.

Uneven Teams

The game is built for an even number of players. In the author's experience the most balanced and the most fun mode is three on three. With an odd number of players, small adjustments even the teams out: the smaller side's starting victory points change, as does the number of cards players take in the draw phase. Detailed balance tables follow.
